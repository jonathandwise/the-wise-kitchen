#chapter Thai Curries
#blurb Red, panang and choo chee curries, from a weeknight chicken curry to whole mackerel.

= Choo Chee Mackerel (Fillets)
serves: 2
from: Hot Thai Kitchen (Pailin Chongchitnant) | hot-thai-kitchen.com
[Ingredients]
- 2 mackerel fillets
- 2–3 tbsp red curry paste
- ¼ cup plus ⅔ cup coconut milk
- 1 tsp finely chopped palm sugar
- 1–2 tsp fish sauce, to taste
- 3 makrut (kaffir) lime leaves, center rib removed, finely julienned
- Vegetable oil, for searing
- Julienned red spur chili or red bell pepper, to garnish (optional)
- 1–2 tbsp extra coconut milk, to garnish (optional)
- Jasmine rice, to serve
[Method]
- Bring ¼ cup coconut milk to a boil in a wok or wide sauté pan.
- Add the curry paste and stir constantly until it's thick and the coconut oil starts to separate (if it doesn't separate, carry on anyway).
- Add the remaining ⅔ cup coconut milk, the palm sugar and fish sauce and simmer a few minutes, until thick and rich but still pourable.
- Stir in half the lime leaves and let them infuse for about a minute. Season the sauce boldly, since it mellows once it's served with the fish and rice. Set aside, covered.
- Pat the fish dry. Heat a thin layer of oil in a large pan over medium-high heat. Sear skin-side down about 2 minutes, until golden, then flip and cook 1 minute more.
- Bring the sauce back to a boil, thinning with a splash of water if needed. Add the fish skin-side up and simmer 30 seconds to 1 minute, keeping the sauce off the crisp skin.
- Plate the fish, pour the sauce around it and over the flesh, then drizzle with the extra coconut milk and top with the rest of the lime leaves and the chili.
[Tips]
- Making your own curry paste? Halve the salt and shrimp paste.

= Choo Chee Mackerel Curry (Whole Fish)
serves: 4
time: Prep 10 min · Cook 15 min
from: Travel Food Atlas | travelfoodatlas.com
[Ingredients]
- 700 g (about 1½ lb) whole fresh mackerel, cleaned
- 3 tbsp vegetable oil
- 3 tbsp Thai red curry paste
- ¾ cup coconut milk, plus a little extra to garnish
- 2 makrut (kaffir) lime leaves, center rib removed, torn
- 2½ tbsp fish sauce
- 50 g (about ¼ cup) palm sugar, or brown or white sugar
- 4 makrut lime leaves, finely shredded, to garnish
- 1 red chili, cut into thin strips, to garnish
- Steamed rice, to serve
[Method]
- Gut and clean the mackerel, rinse well and pat dry.
- Heat the oil in a wide pan over medium heat and fry the curry paste for 1–2 minutes.
- Add half the coconut milk and fry until fragrant, then add the torn lime leaves, fish sauce and sugar and stir.
- When it boils, add the rest of the coconut milk and bring back to a boil over high heat.
- Add the mackerel and turn the heat to medium-low. Spoon the sauce over the fish (a wide pan keeps the fish from being drowned). Cover and cook 15 minutes.
- Uncover and simmer 3–5 minutes more to thicken the sauce, basting the fish again.
- Garnish with the shredded lime leaves, chili and a drizzle of coconut milk, and serve with steamed rice.

= Panang Curry
serves: 5
time: Prep 15 min · Cook 25 min
from: Tastes Better From Scratch | tastesbetterfromscratch.com
[Ingredients]
- 1 tbsp oil
- 2 tbsp Panang curry paste
- 1 tbsp peanut butter
- 2 lb chicken breasts, cut across the grain into bite-size pieces
- 1 small onion, sliced
- 1 green bell pepper, thinly sliced
- 1 red bell pepper, thinly sliced
- 2 tsp grated fresh ginger
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 cans (14 oz each) coconut milk
- 2 tsp cornstarch
- ¼ cup packed light brown sugar
- 1 tbsp fish sauce
- 1 tbsp lime juice
- 1 cup loosely packed basil leaves, roughly chopped
- Salt and pepper
- Cooked rice, to serve
[Method]
- Heat the oil in a large nonstick skillet over medium-high heat. Cook the onion 2 minutes, then add the peppers, garlic and ginger and cook 2 minutes more.
- Stir in the curry paste and peanut butter for 1 minute. Add 1½ cans of the coconut milk. Whisk the cornstarch into the remaining half can and stir it in.
- Add the chicken and simmer 10–15 minutes, until cooked through and the sauce begins to thicken.
- Stir in the brown sugar, fish sauce, lime juice and basil and simmer 5 minutes. Season with salt and pepper and serve over rice.
[Tips]
- Mae Ploy paste is recommended. 2 tbsp is medium-hot; add chili sauce at the end if you want more heat.
- Mushrooms, zucchini, bamboo shoots, spinach or carrots all work, and so do shrimp or tofu instead of chicken.
- Keeps 5–7 days in the fridge or 2–3 months in the freezer.

= Salmon Coconut Curry
serves: 4
time: Prep 15 min · Cook 20 min
from: Salt & Lavender | saltandlavender.com
[Ingredients]
- 1 lb salmon fillet
- Salt and pepper
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tbsp butter
- ½ medium onion, chopped
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 heaping tbsp Thai red curry paste
- ½ tsp fish sauce (optional but recommended)
- 1 can (13.5 oz) full-fat coconut milk
- ½ tsp brown sugar
- ½ cup matchstick carrots
- 1 heaping cup chopped broccolini
- 1 tsp lime juice
- 2 tbsp torn fresh basil
- 2 tbsp chopped fresh cilantro
[Method]
- Cut the salmon into 4 pieces, pat dry and season with salt and pepper.
- Heat the oil and butter in a skillet over medium-high heat. Sear the salmon skin-side down for 5 minutes (let it release on its own), then flip and cook 2–3 minutes more, until almost done. Move it to a plate and slip off the skin if you like.
- Pour off all but about 1 tbsp of the fat. Cook the onion about 3 minutes, until lightly browned, then stir in the garlic and curry paste for 1 minute.
- Add the fish sauce, coconut milk, brown sugar, carrots and broccolini and simmer gently about 5 minutes, until the vegetables are tender-crisp.
- Stir in the lime juice, nestle the salmon back in and warm through for a couple of minutes. Season to taste and top with the basil and cilantro.
[Tips]
- Thai Kitchen curry paste and coconut milk are recommended; that brand's coconut milk is extra thick.
- Soy sauce can stand in for fish sauce, though the flavor changes. Regular broccoli works if you cut it small.

= Thai Red Curry with Chicken
serves: 4
time: Prep 5 min · Cook 20 min
from: RecipeTin Eats | recipetineats.com
[Ingredients: Curry paste (choose one)]
- 5–6 tbsp store-bought Thai red curry paste (Maesri recommended)
- or 1 batch homemade red curry paste
[Ingredients: Extras, for jar paste only]
- 2 large garlic cloves, minced
- 2 tsp finely grated fresh ginger
- 1 tbsp lemongrass paste (or 2 tsp finely minced fresh lemongrass)
[Ingredients: Curry]
- 3 tbsp vegetable oil
- 1 cup (250 ml) low-sodium chicken broth
- 1 can (400 ml / 14 oz) full-fat coconut milk
- 6 makrut (kaffir) lime leaves
- 1 tbsp sugar (white, brown or palm)
- 2 tsp fish sauce, plus more to taste
- 350 g (12 oz) boneless, skinless chicken thighs, sliced about ⅓ inch thick
- 150 g (5 oz) pumpkin or butternut squash, in 1.5 cm (generous ½-inch) cubes
- 120 g (4 oz) green beans, cut into 2-inch pieces
- 12 Thai basil leaves
- Sliced red chili, cilantro and jasmine rice, to serve
[Method]
- Heat the oil in a large heavy skillet over medium-high heat.
- Add the curry paste (and the extras, if using jar paste) and cook about 2 minutes to dry it out.
- Pour in the broth, stir to dissolve the paste and simmer hard for 3 minutes, until reduced by half.
- Stir in the coconut milk, lime leaves, sugar and fish sauce, then add the chicken and spread it out.
- Bring to a simmer, lower the heat to medium and cook 8–10 minutes, until the chicken is done and the sauce has reduced.
- Taste: add fish sauce for saltiness or sugar for sweetness.
- Add the squash and green beans and cook 3 minutes, until the squash is just tender and the sauce has thickened.
- Off the heat, stir in the basil. Serve over jasmine rice with chili and cilantro.
[Tips]
- Jar paste is much stronger than homemade. With Maesri, 5 tbsp is medium-hot and 6 tbsp is spicy.
- A little oil pooling on the surface is normal for Thai curry.
- Thigh stays juiciest, but breast or tenderloin also works. Regular basil can stand in for Thai basil; use just a small handful.
