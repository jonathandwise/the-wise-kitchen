= Chef John's Swedish Meatballs
serves: 6
time: Prep 30 min, plus 1 hr chilling · Cook 45 min
temp: 425°F / 220°C
from: Allrecipes · recipe by John Mitzewich (Chef John) | allrecipes.com
[Ingredients: Meatballs]
- 2 tbsp butter
- ½ yellow onion, finely chopped
- 1½ tsp fine salt
- 2 large eggs
- ⅓ cup plain breadcrumbs
- ¼ cup milk
- ¾ tsp freshly ground black pepper
- ¼ tsp ground nutmeg
- ¼ tsp ground allspice
- Pinch of cayenne
- 1 lb ground beef chuck
- 1 lb ground pork
- Cooking spray
[Ingredients: Gravy]
- 2 tbsp butter
- 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 3¼ cups beef broth
- ½ cup heavy cream
- ½ tsp sugar
- ¼ tsp Worcestershire sauce
- Salt and black pepper
[Method]
- **Meatballs:** Melt the butter in a large skillet over medium heat and cook the onion with the salt about 6 minutes, until translucent.
- Tip the onion into a large bowl and stir in the eggs, breadcrumbs, milk, pepper, nutmeg, allspice and cayenne. Mix in the beef and pork until evenly combined. Cover and refrigerate 1 hour.
- **Gravy:** Melt the butter in a large skillet over medium heat, whisk in the flour and cook 4–5 minutes, until golden. Slowly whisk in the broth. Raise the heat to medium-high, whisk in the cream and bring to a simmer. Stir in the sugar and Worcestershire and simmer 6–7 minutes, until slightly thickened. Season and set aside off the heat.
- Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Line a baking sheet with foil and coat it lightly with cooking spray.
- With wet hands, roll the mixture into balls of 2–3 tbsp each and set them on the sheet.
- Bake about 20 minutes, until browned and 160°F (71°C) in the center.
- Add the meatballs to the gravy and warm through over medium-low heat, about 5 minutes.

= Grilled Santa Maria Tri-Tip
serves: 8–10
time: 40 min, plus at least 1 hr in the rub
from: NYT Cooking · recipe by Kim Severson | cooking.nytimes.com
note: Tri-tip is the boomerang-shaped roast at the heart of Santa Maria barbecue. You may have to ask a butcher or the store's meat counter to order one. Two pounds is a good size, but grab a bigger one if you see it; leftovers make excellent sandwiches.
[Ingredients]
- 1 whole tri-tip (about 2 lb)
- 3 tbsp beef rub of your choice
[Method]
- Trim off the silver skin. If there's a thick layer of fat, slice some of it away, but leave plenty to baste the meat as it cooks.
- Before seasoning, look at which way the long muscle fibers run on each end of the roast. The grain changes direction partway along, and you'll want to know where when it's time to carve.
- Sprinkle the rub all over the meat and massage it in lightly. Cover and refrigerate for at least an hour, or as long as overnight. Take it out of the fridge an hour before cooking.
- Build a charcoal fire or heat a gas grill to high. Sear the roast until the first side is well browned, 6–8 minutes, watching for flare-ups. Turn it and sear the other side for about as long.
- Turn the gas down to medium-high, or move the roast to a cooler part of the charcoal grill. Turn it again and cook 8–10 minutes, then flip and keep cooking. A 2-lb roast takes about 20–25 minutes in all. It's ready when an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part reads 130°F (54°C).
- Rest the roast on a cutting board for 10–20 minutes. Cut it in two at the bend, where the grain changes direction, then slice each piece thinly against the grain.
[Tips]
- Any beef rub works here. Traditional Santa Maria seasoning keeps it simple: mostly salt, pepper and garlic.

= Mississippi Pot Roast
time: Slow cook 8 hr on Low
from: The Country Cook | thecountrycook.net
[Ingredients]
- 2 tbsp olive or vegetable oil
- 2–3 lb chuck roast
- Salt and pepper
- 1 packet ranch dressing mix
- 1 packet dry onion soup mix (or au jus gravy mix)
- ½ cup (1 stick) salted butter
- 8 pepperoncini peppers
[Method]
- Heat the oil in a large skillet over high heat (or use your slow cooker's sear setting).
- Pat the roast dry, season both sides with salt and pepper, and sear 2–3 minutes per side.
- Move it to the slow cooker. Sprinkle the ranch mix and onion soup mix over the top, set the butter on the roast and tuck the pepperoncini on and around it.
- Cover and cook on Low for 8 hours, without lifting the lid.
- Shred the meat with two forks, discard any large fatty pieces, and serve with the juices.
[Tips]
- Don't cook it on High; it needs the long, low cook.
- Every time the lid comes off you lose heat and moisture, so leave it closed.

= Pan-Seared Rib-Eye
serves: 1–2
time: Prep 10 min · Cook 5 min
temp: 500°F / 260°C
from: Alton Brown, Food Network | foodnetwork.com
[Ingredients]
- 1 boneless rib-eye steak, 1½ inches thick
- Canola oil, to coat
- Kosher salt and black pepper
[Method]
- Put a 10–12-inch cast-iron skillet in the oven and heat it to 500°F (260°C). Let the steak come to room temperature while it heats.
- When the oven is hot, move the skillet to the stovetop over high heat for 5 minutes. Lightly oil the steak and season both sides generously with salt and pepper.
- Lay the steak in the center of the dry, very hot skillet and leave it alone for 30 seconds. Flip and sear 30 seconds more.
- Move the pan to the oven for 2 minutes, flip the steak, and give it 2 minutes more for medium-rare. For medium, add 1 minute to each side.
- Tent loosely with foil and rest 2 minutes. Serve whole or thinly sliced.
[Tips]
- The skillet handle stays dangerously hot after it comes out of the oven, so keep a mitt on it.
- Expect smoke. Turn on the vent fan before the steak goes in.

= Peruvian Lomo Saltado
serves: 4
time: Prep 15 min · Cook 20 min
from: Tasty | tasty.co
[Ingredients]
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil, divided
- 1 lb (455 g) sirloin steak, cut into strips
- Salt and pepper
- ½ red onion, sliced
- 1 medium tomato, sliced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tbsp ají amarillo paste
- 2 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp white vinegar
- 1 tbsp chopped fresh cilantro
- 1 lb (455 g) french fries, cooked and hot
- Cooked white rice, to serve (optional)
[Method]
- Heat 1 tbsp of the oil in a large pan over high heat. Add the steak, season with salt and pepper and sear until browned, 5–6 minutes. Transfer to a plate.
- Add the remaining oil and the onion to the pan and cook about 5 minutes, until soft and browned.
- Add the tomato, garlic and ají amarillo paste and cook 5–7 minutes, until the tomato has released some juice but still holds its shape.
- Stir in the soy sauce and vinegar and cook 1 minute.
- Return the steak, add the fries and cilantro and toss gently to coat everything in the sauce. Serve with rice if you like.

= Reverse-Seared Steak
serves: 2
time: Prep 5 min · Oven about 20–40 min
temp: 200–275°F / 93–135°C
from: Serious Eats · recipe by J. Kenji López-Alt | seriouseats.com
note: Low and slow first, sear last: the steak comes out evenly cooked from edge to edge, with a crisp crust and no need to rest.
[Ingredients]
- Thick-cut steak(s), 1½–2 inches thick, such as rib-eye, strip, porterhouse, T-bone, tri-tip or filet mignon
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 1 tbsp vegetable oil (for the stovetop finish)
- 1 tbsp butter (for the stovetop finish)
[Method: Oven and skillet]
- Season the steaks generously all over with salt and pepper. If you have time, set them on a wire rack in a rimmed baking sheet and refrigerate overnight, uncovered, to dry the surface.
- Heat the oven to anywhere from 200 to 275°F (93–135°C). An even lower setting works too; it just takes longer.
- Set the steaks on a wire rack in a rimmed baking sheet and cook until an instant-read thermometer reads 105°F (41°C) for rare, 115°F (46°C) for medium-rare, 125°F (52°C) for medium or 135°F (57°C) for medium-well. Figure about 20 minutes for rare and up to 40 for medium-well, but times vary a lot, so check often.
- Just before the steaks are done, heat the oil in a cast-iron, carbon-steel or heavy stainless skillet over high heat until it smokes. Add the steaks and the butter and sear until well browned, about 45 seconds per side. Hold each steak on its side with tongs to sear the edges, then serve right away.
[Method: On the grill]
- Season the steaks as above. Light a full chimney of charcoal, and when the coals are covered in gray ash, spread them over half the grate (on a gas grill, turn half the burners to high). Set the cooking grate in place, cover and preheat for 5 minutes, then clean and oil the grate.
- Cook the steaks on the cooler side, uncovered, turning now and then, until they reach the same temperatures as above. Check them often.
- Move the steaks to a platter and tent with foil. Build the charcoal fire up as hot as you can (or turn every gas burner to high and preheat with the lid closed), then sear the steaks over the hottest part, turning often, until crisp and charred all over, about 1½ minutes total. Serve right away.

= Rosemary Braised Lamb Shanks
serves: 6
time: Prep 20 min · Cook 3 hr 5 min
from: Allrecipes · recipe by S Hodge | allrecipes.com
[Ingredients]
- 6 lamb shanks
- Salt and pepper
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 3 large carrots, sliced into ¼-inch rounds
- 2 onions, chopped
- 10 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 bottle (750 ml) red wine
- 1 can (28 oz) whole peeled tomatoes, with juice
- 1 can (10.5 oz) condensed chicken broth
- 1 can (10.5 oz) beef broth
- 5 tsp chopped fresh rosemary
- 2 tsp chopped fresh thyme
[Method]
- Season the shanks with salt and pepper.
- Heat the oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Brown the shanks on all sides in batches, about 8 minutes, then set them aside on a plate.
- Lower the heat to medium, add the carrots, onions and garlic and cook about 10 minutes, until golden, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom.
- Stir in the wine, tomatoes, both broths, the rosemary and thyme.
- Nestle the shanks back into the liquid and bring to a boil. Turn the heat to medium-low, cover and simmer about 2 hours, until the lamb is tender.
- Uncover and simmer 20 minutes more to concentrate the flavor. Move the shanks to a platter and keep them warm under foil.
- Boil the sauce about 15 minutes, until thickened, and spoon it over the shanks.
[Tips]
- For a one-pot meal, add 1 lb small potatoes to the pot for the last 45 minutes instead of serving with mash.

= Slow Roasted Rabbit
serves: 4
time: Prep 20 min · Cook 1 hr 35 min
temp: 350°F / 175°C
from: Allrecipes · recipe by Jane T | allrecipes.com
[Ingredients]
- 1 rabbit (about 3 lb), cleaned and cut into pieces
- 1 tbsp black pepper
- 1¾ tsp salt
- ¼ cup vegetable oil
- 1 onion, chopped
- 1 cup water
- ¾ cup ketchup
- 1½ tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 4 tsp sugar
- 1 tbsp paprika
- 1 garlic clove, chopped
[Method]
- Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C).
- Season the rabbit with the pepper and salt.
- Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat and brown the rabbit on all sides. Move it to a 9 × 13 baking dish.
- Stir together the onion, water, ketchup, Worcestershire, sugar, paprika and garlic and pour it over the rabbit.
- Bake uncovered about 90 minutes, basting often, until very tender and at least 160°F (71°C) near the bone.
